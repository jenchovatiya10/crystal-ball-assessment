# Crystal Ball Approval Policy

## Review Order

Reviewers must process the approval queue by urgency, not by submission time alone. Overdue items come first, then items due within forty-eight hours, then the remainder. Within the same urgency band, safety-tagged content outranks routine demos. Deterministic ranking already encodes this order; reviewers explain and act on that order rather than inventing a personal sequence. When two items look similar, prefer the earlier due date and document why in the review note.

## Item Type Rules

Each fixture type has a focused checklist. Folders such as Site Patrol Onboarding & Checklists require confirming nested checklist completeness and access prerequisites. Videos such as the Level 2 Drone Patrol Video Demo need a full playback pass for hazard cues and narrative accuracy. PDFs such as Safety Equipment & Sensor Specs require figure and table verification against the linked sensors. Images such as the 360° Spatial Zone Layout & Camera Map need coverage validation for every marked camera zone. Do not approve a type without completing its type-specific checks.

## Escalation

Escalate when a reviewer lacks credentials, the asset is corrupt, or policy conflicts block a decision. Route escalations to the duty supervisor within one business day and keep the original approval ID in the ticket. Do not silently reassign ownership outside the escalation path. If safety flags are present and unresolved, escalate immediately rather than waiting for the standard window. Record the escalation timestamp and contact in the approval notes.

## Rejection and Notes

Reject only with a concrete, actionable note that cites the failing checklist item. Vague notes such as "needs work" are not acceptable. When rejecting a folder, video, pdf, or image, name the section or timestamp that failed. Approvals that pass should still receive a short confirmation note so audit trails remain complete. Reviewers must never alter ranking scores; notes explain the supplied ranking and the decision only.

## SLA Guidance

Initial review should begin within one business day of submission for standard items and within four business hours for safety-flagged or overdue work. Final disposition targets remain two business days unless escalation is open. If an SLA breach is likely, notify the requester and update the due-risk flag before the deadline. Queue health is measured by overdue count and median time-to-decision; empty grounded answers are preferable to guessing when policy context is missing.
